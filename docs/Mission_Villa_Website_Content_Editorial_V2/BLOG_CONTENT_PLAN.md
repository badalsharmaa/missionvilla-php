## Suggested article structure
- A plain-English answer in the first two paragraphs.
- Practical sections responding directly to the headline question.
- A short, helpful checklist or takeaway only when relevant.
- Clearly distinguish general educational information from **Mission Villa-specific** services/policies.
- One contextual link back to the most relevant service page, plus one appropriate contact/visit action.
- For clinical or legal issues, use qualified source review and appropriate disclaimers. Avoid diagnosing, promising outcomes, or replacing medical advice.

## Editorial sourcing rules
- Audit old blog articles before reusing them. Check original author, date, permissions, factual currency and medical information.
- Verify all references to Alzheimer's, dementia, mobility, clinical eligibility, and care transitions against credible current sources and community policy.
- Avoid fake staff quotes, unsourced effectiveness statistics, guarantees, or assumed Medicare/Medicaid/insurance acceptance.
- Do not invent article URLs, images, CMS IDs, bylines, publication dates or live status.
- Write articles for actual visitors first; SEO titles, headings and internal links should help discoverability without turning pages into keyword dumps.
- Blog/article designs can be longer and more detailed because visitors opt into reading them. Core service pages should stay short.

## Content writer deliverables (for each article)
1. Draft headline and intended reader question.
2. 600–1,000 words **only if the topic requires that depth**; do not force a quota.
3. A short research/source list and a fact-check checklist.
4. SEO title and meta description.
5. 1–2 relevant links to actual Mission Villa pages.
6. Explicit status: draft / client review / approved / published.
